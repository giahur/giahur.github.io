Interlinked

3-D First-Person Action Game

Role: Gameplay and AI Developer
Team Size: 13 people
Duration: 4 months
Unreal Engine 5 C++ Blueprints Gameplay Ability System AI Behavior Trees Procedural Animation Physical Materials

Play as a divine soldier. Armed with six spells themed around flesh and technology, kill three distinct enemy types to harvest enhancements.

Process

Overview

Interlinked is a 3D first-person action game built in Unreal Engine 5. This project was built across three milestones as part of a graduate-level game production course. Players could cast six spells including Digitize, Volt Cascade, Life Dash, Contagion, Flesh Wall, and Lockjaw. Levels were populated with three enemy archetypes: CCG, Torture Nexus, and Meatloaf. The final deliverable was a playable demo that served as a tutorial level, introducing the spells, enemies, and enhancement system.

Milestone 1: Core Loop and Scoping

The team entered this course with a very ambitious scope. We had initially planned for six enemies, six spells, and multiple levels. After discussion with the 3D artists, this was cut down to 3 enemies. I, along with the rest of the development team, was responsible for the six spells. At first, we set up SpellBase as a parent class to the 6 spells.

However, the team ran into issues where spells needed one blueprint to hold its initial logic and a separate blueprint for its static mesh. This led us to instead create a manager for each of the spells, which were in turn children of the SpellManager. The managers then call the static mesh of the spells. This solution was messy, and each individual spell manager had to call the parent SpellManager, repeatedly calling logic unnecessarily for each individual spell.

After doing some research, we decided to use Unreal Engine 5's Gameplay Ability System (GAS). This reduced implementation overhead, standardizing ability activation, cooldowns, gameplay effects, attributes, and cues. I wrote internal documentation to onboard the rest of the development team. I also implemented the Flesh Wall spell. This spell was a placeable wall that expired after a set duration. The spell was implemented using gameplay effects, tags, cues, and attributes as well as cooldown handling, collision logic and actor destruction.

Milestone 2: AI Solo Behaviors

In this milestone, the development team began implementing the AI enemies based on the design team's specifications.

I began with the enemy Meatloaf, a tank-like creature with slow movement and projectiles. When alone, the enemy patrols the area for the player, pursuing and repeatedly shooting the player upon seeing or hearing them. These behaviors were implemented using behavior trees and AI perception. The large plates on Meatloaf's torso protect the front and back, leaving him vulnerable on the sides. This was implemented with Unreal Engine's Physical Materials.

Next, I assisted with the movement animations for the Torture Nexus (TN) enemy. This enemy is made up of a toso and head on top of spider-like mechanical legs. It is constantly in pain, represented by its erratic movements. Originally, our 3D artists had intended on creating the animations for all enemies. However, TN's frantic walking and ability to move in all directions proved to be difficult to implement. After some research, the team determined that this would be best implemented using Procedural Animation with a Locomotor. In the enemy's Control Rig, I combined the Locomotor to generate pelvis motion with Full Body IK to generate leg poses. After adjusting the Phase Offset, Movement and Stepping Tuning, and collisions, we were able to create a walking animation for TN that aligned with its creepy, crawly design. TN may also jump to the ceiling to attack the player. With some adjustments, the locomotor was able to procedurally animate the legs so that TN appeared to be climbing to the ceiling.

Milestone 3: AI Group Behaviors and Polish

In the final milestone, the development continued working on AI behavior trees, now focusing on group behavior. When Meatloafs see each other, they will assign the centermost one as the leader. The other Meatloafs will then form a line, flanking the leader Meatloaf. Then, as a unit, the Meatloafs will patrol and chase the player. If Meatloafs encounter another of their kind or one in formation dies, they will send a signal to the leader to reform the group. To see the detailed Blueprints, see the "Code" section below.

The team also focused on polishing the game through whiteboxing and bug fixes. After AI enemy group behaviors were finalized, we began coding formations across enemny factions, as well as enemy affiliations with each other. We also implemented all models, materials, and animations for the player, spells, and enemies.
