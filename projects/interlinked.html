Interlinked

3-D First-Person Roguelite

Unreal Engine 5 C# Blueprints
Role: Developer
Team Size: 13 people
Duration: 4 months

Play as two characters, a Witch and her Bear familiar, to navigate environments while avoiding enemies. Use the Witch's spells to manipulate the environment and the Bear's strength to incapacitate enemies and push obstacles. Work together to reach the exit!

Responsibilities

Designer

  • Designed 8 of the game's 11 levels, each a puzzle introducing a specific mechanic.
  • Conceived and designed the game's items and abilities, including the Cosmic Shroud (invisibility for one turn) and Honey Jar (two movements in one turn).

Developer

  • Programmed the Honey and Cosmic Shroud functionality.
  • Built and hooked up UI screens, including the pause menu, level select screen, and lose screen.
  • Resolved UI issues across development, such as inventory stretching, button hierarchy, and inconsistencies in phases.
  • Integrated updated sprites and refactored code to include directional logic.
  • Updated cloud sprite placement logic.
  • Updated and debugged the Bear's action turn flow.
  • Implemented the enemy hover interaction to display sightlines.

Process

Ursa Magus was built in Unity 6 over a period of four weeks. Each level functions as a self-contained puzzle, introducing mechanics incrementally. Abilities and items each have a dedicated tutorial level to introduce players to the different mechanics.
In week 1, we set up the basic grid system, with players controlling the Witch and Bear in a simple level with one enemy. After the first playtest, we received valuable feedback that players had a lot of confusion around the tile movement, phase structure, and enemy sightlines. We identified that tutorials would be a necessary next step to onboard players.
We also asked players for their insights on future character ability design, and found that they aligned with the team's plans. This indicated to us that the theming and styling of the game matched player expectations.
In week 2, playtesters were more familiar with the turn-based game structure and were able to give more feedback on the level design. Levels were often too open, allowing players to reach the exit without using the intended ability or item. Some levels were too reliant on either the Witch or Bear, going against the cooperative puzzle-solving aspect of the game. Lastly, players were introduced to the "Bear Hug" ability first, which incapacitates enemies. Because of this, players believed that all enemies had to be defeated to beat the levels, discouraging them from using other abilities or items.
I redesigned levels based on this feedback. As a team, we decided on the order in which we wanted to introduce abilities and spells. I then reworked the levels so that players were forced to use a given ability or item to beat a level. This often meant blocking the enemy from the Bear's reach, or trapping the player with the enemy.
In week 3, we implemented our cloud ability, which allows players to clear gaps in the level, and our tutorial system. Playtesters responded very positively to the new onboarding through tutorials and clearer level design. Players began using their spells and items to beat the levels in clever ways, as opposed to defaulting to any single one.
In our final sprint, we focused on bugs and implementing updated assets. Leads reported to the producer; team members reported to their subteam lead.* --- ## Project Description InterLinked is a 3D first-person roguelite built in Unreal Engine 5 over two milestones as part of a graduate-level game production course. Players take the role of a divine soldier inside the "Divine Apparatus" — a god-machine under attack by a spreading VIRUS infection. Armed with six spells themed around flesh and technology, players navigate a series of interconnected areas, kill enemies to harvest organic and tech materials, and spend those materials at an NPC shopkeeper on persistent upgrades. The spell kit is built around synergy. Spells include **Digitize** (rapid-fire with damage ramp), **Volt Cascade** (chain lightning), **Life Dash** (invincibility dash that steals health on pass-through), **Contagion** (AoE debuff zone), **Flesh Wall** (placeable cover that explodes on death), and **Lockjaw** (pulls enemies together). Players are intended to chain these — wall enemies off, cluster them, hit them with cascade, finish with contagion. Three enemy archetypes populate the levels: **CCG** (fast, cowardly scout), **Torture Nexus** (slow sniper), and **Meatloaf** (armored tank with formation synergies). The looting and upgrade system is designed for roguelite replayability — basic enhancements are run-specific, while major structural upgrades (e.g., wall-walking spider legs) persist across deaths. The final deliverable was a playable demo covering the onboarding area and first system of the Apparatus, including all six spells, three enemy types, and a connected level sequence. --- ## Our Process — Iteration Across Three Milestones **Milestone 1 — Core Loop & Scope Reckoning** The team entered production with ambitious scope: six enemies, six spells, a boss, a shop, and a full onboarding sequence. Within the first few weeks it became clear this was not achievable. The team cut from six enemies to four with a boss, then later cut the boss and boss room entirely. The playtest target shifted to a sandbox space for testing combat feel rather than a complete experience. Despite the cuts, the spell side overdelivered: six spells were implemented where three were originally planned. This was largely enabled by the discovery of GAS — once the system was integrated and documented, the per-spell implementation cost dropped significantly, and additional spells became feasible within the milestone window. The major process issue in this milestone was pre-production delays. Cross-team communication was sparse, as class time was used for subteam meetings rather than whole-team syncs. This became the team's most persistent structural problem. **Milestone 2 — AI, Level Design & Playtest Feedback** The second milestone introduced enemy behaviors, level design, and full playtest cycles. Playtest feedback was extensive and shaped the development backlog directly: *Level design* feedback identified that defensive abilities were all introduced before offensive ones, creating a pacing problem. Rooms were too small for certain abilities to read correctly (Lockjaw's area needed to be larger; the Meatloaf room needed the enemy to stay centered). Players were consistently reaching unintended areas (escaping bounds via Flesh Wall), which the team addressed with collision adjustments. *UI/UX* feedback was consistent across all playtesters: ability icons were too small, cooldown states were hard to read, and the fill-up animation was confusing. A persistent bug caused the cooldown timer to go negative. Players also couldn't tell when abilities had recharged. *Enemy feedback* flagged that dead body colliders were blocking shots and pathfinding — a high-frequency complaint. CCGs were not reacting to being attacked, and Torture Nexus was perceived as too difficult with hitscan attacks. *Flesh Wall specifically* generated the most feedback of any single mechanic: players consistently aimed at walls instead of the floor, the cooldown felt punishing on a miss, the controls were unintuitive on controller, and the wall's disappearance was abrupt with no warning. On the positive side, players discovered and enjoyed using it as a movement tool (platforming, flanking cover), which the team leaned into as an intended secondary use. **Milestone 3 — Polish & Integration** The final milestone focused on bringing systems to a more complete state rather than adding new content. The postmortem identified that the game was hitting approximately 50–60% of the potential of each of its systems — a diagnosis shared independently by both playtester feedback and the faculty reviewer. The team's reflection acknowledged that beginning spell and enemy implementation without a fully locked narrative direction caused some cohesion issues, and that a vertical slice approach (building the game chronologically rather than by mechanic) may have produced a tighter result. --- ## Technical Notes - **Gameplay Ability System (GAS)**: All spells are implemented as GAS Gameplay Abilities. Cooldowns and damage-over-time are managed through Gameplay Effects, which are duplicated from a base template and configured per-spell. This architecture allowed new spells to be added with minimal boilerplate. - **Behavior Trees & Blackboards**: Enemy AI runs on UE5 Behavior Trees with custom Behavior Tree Tasks written in Blueprint. Meatloaf formation behavior required solving group movement without excessive math — solutions informed in part by consultation with faculty (David Sanchez) on collision layering to prevent AI clipping while maintaining group cohesion. - **Version Control**: The team used GitHub with Unreal Engine, which caused recurring merge conflicts on binary `.uasset` files. This was an unresolved workflow issue throughout production. --- **Tools Used** Unreal Engine 5 · Blueprint · C++ · GAS (Gameplay Ability System) · GitHub · Trello · Google Drive · Canva · Pinterest
