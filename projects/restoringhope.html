Restoring Hope (Class Choice Award)

2-D narrative-driven puzzle game about a young girl fixing up her castle.

Role: Puzzle Lead and Developer
Team Size: 9 people
Duration: 3 months
Unity C#

Play as a pretty princess fixing up her castle with her magic wand. The more she fixes, the more... things go wrong? Explore her castle and investigate strange discrepancies to learn the truth behind the sparkles and magic.

Play Design Doc

Responsibilities

Puzzle Lead and Developer

  • Led puzzle design and development by conceptualizing and implementing several core puzzles, delegating design and development work, and approving ideas and implementation.
  • Organized game flow, defining puzzle locations and conditions, collectibles, and item interactions.
  • Updated narrative elements and created puzzles to support story progression.
  • Worked closely with artists to implement UI elements, room layouts, animations, and collectibles.
  • Prepared promotional and release assets.

Lock Puzzle

Since this is a narrative-driven game, we wanted to keep the puzzles relatively easy, but themed to support the story. From the beginning, we knew we wanted to establish the main character Maeve and have her diary be the first thing that players interacted with, but had several options for the puzzle aspect. Some ideas were deemed too hard (using months to denote the numbers), and others too easy (having the numbers directly in her entry), until we landed on the idea of using hidden shapes in Maeve's diary to hint at the chest's code.

This simple mechanic was also a way to set up the style of puzzles throughout the game, where the answers for puzzles can lie in other interactions. The diary also established Maeve's character as a young girl (with child-like handwriting, mispellings) whose mother is sick, but she doesn't fully understand the circumstances. After solving this puzzle, her magic wand is added to your inventory. The wand is used to reveal the rest of the puzzles throughout the game. The player has limited charges of her wand, with a cooldown timer, and going over will make too much noise and wake her dad. This helped prevent players from spamming the wand in order to find the hidden puzzles.

TV Signal Puzzle

As we continue the story, Maeve is slowly discovering that her castle is actually an ordinary home, disguised by her imagination. Due to this, we had to design around castle elements that closely resemble common household items. This next puzzle is revealed when the player hovers their wand over the fireplace. The fireplace puzzle opens, revealing a box TV. We wanted players to have some questions on why the element appears differently in the puzzle, but not so much so that they seem unrelated, which is why the TV has a fire animation playing on the screen.

In keeping with the idea of simple puzzle designs, we designed this puzzle to be as intuitive as possible. Players must use the static on the TV to determine the randomized goal placement of the TV antennas.

Garden Puzzle

This puzzle was a sliding puzzle, in which players slide pieces to create the final picture (the layout of the garden). In playtests, we received feedback that since many of the pieces had similar elements, it was difficult to determine the correct order. Based on this feedback, we put subtle, colored flowers in the bushes to help players line up different pieces. In the next playtest, we had conflicted feedback with about half of players finding it too difficult, and others too easy. Afer discussion with my developers, we decided to add a puzzle difficulty slider in the menu, along with respective variations of each puzzle.

Chandelier Puzzle

This puzzle was first designed to be a simple circuit box, where players had to switch on breakers and determine which appliance they corresponded to. However, in early playtests, players found this task to be tedious and not fun. The team pivoted to a simple wire puzzle, based on the classic "Among Us" minigame task. Rather than simply matching colors, we kept the theme of matching energy requirements to different appliances.

Instead of a guess-and-check puzzle, players must interact with the different appliances in the kitchen. This was based on a simplified version of logic grid puzzles. Each appliance has a certain energy requirement relative to another appliance. Based on all the information given, the player must solve the connections.
